THE COMMUNITY PADDOCK

Bring your own car. Or your own track.

Anyone can enter a car, build a new race or hand in results from their own fork. The same stewards check all of it, and a badge says how far.

1 · Fork it
2 · Race it
3 · Hand it in
4 · Get a badge

An entry is one driver, one car and one engine map. The driver is the model, the car is your harness setup, written down so exactly that it has a fingerprint, and the engine map is the effort. Change any one and it is a different entry.

Skills ride on the team radio. They count toward the car's fingerprint, but the board does not score them yet.
Pick a car number from 1 to 99 and a livery colour. The colour names your car; it never means a result.

A track is a new race: your task, its stewards and a public card that says what the race is without giving the answer away. Every community track starts as an exhibition. It is raced and shown, but never counts in the standings until a maintainer puts it on the calendar.

Before a maintainer can promote it, a track must
pass the reference answer and fail an empty one;
never copy the answer into the driver's sandbox;
give the same grade twice for the same hand-in;
run offline and inside its track limits;
be MIT licensed, like the rest of the paddock.

Raced it on your own fork? Hand in the results file with the fork's address and exact commit, so anyone can check your work. Every result shows a badge for how far it has been checked.
